\chapter{Results}

\section{Algorithm Performance Progression}

\subsection{Simple Genetic Algorithm Results}

SGA successfully solved the basic straight-line translation problem, achieving consistent docking within the 25-meter approach distance. Learning curves demonstrated steady fitness improvement over generations, with networks converging to reliable translation and stopping behaviors.

However, SGA failed to solve the diagonal movement problem despite extended training periods. Fitness evolution showed initial improvement followed by premature convergence, with final trajectories demonstrating single-axis solutions rather than coordinated multi-axis control. While extended training might eventually yield solutions, the convergence rate suggested prohibitive computational requirements.

\subsection{Island Model Improvements}

The island model variant improved SGA convergence speed on the basic problem through enhanced diversity maintenance. Migration frequency experiments revealed trade-offs between rapid convergence and final solution quality, with infrequent migration yielding superior average fitness through better diversity preservation.

Despite these improvements, the island model also failed on the diagonal problem, indicating that diversity maintenance alone was insufficient to overcome the sparse reward structure and local optima challenges.

\section{NEAT Performance and Breakthrough}

\subsection{Diagonal Problem Success}

NEAT achieved breakthrough performance on the diagonal movement problem after implementing the LQR-based cost function with shaping rewards. Networks demonstrated coordinated multi-axis control, successfully navigating from origin (0,0) to target (10,10) while maintaining docking constraints.

Trajectory analysis revealed that successful networks did not employ the expected aim-then-translate strategy. Instead, they developed integrated rotation-translation behaviors from mission start, suggesting emergent rather than decomposed control strategies.

\subsection{Rotating Target Limitations}

NEAT failed to solve the full rotating target problem within computational constraints. However, partial success was achieved on simplified scenarios with reduced target distance (3 meters) and angular velocity. These solutions demonstrated the algorithm's capability to learn rotational matching behaviors under favorable conditions.

Networks trained on the simplified rotating problem showed curriculum learning benefits, successfully transferring skills from diagonal → aiming → simplified rotating progression stages.

\section{Network Architecture Analysis}

\subsection{Topology Examination}

Analysis of successful network topologies revealed no obvious logical structure or interpretable control architecture. The evolved networks exhibited complex, seemingly random connectivity patterns rather than expected modular designs for translation and rotation control.

This architectural opacity suggests that NEAT evolved black-box solutions rather than learning explicit behavioral decomposition. The lack of clear structure likely contributed to difficulties in scaling to more complex scenarios.
